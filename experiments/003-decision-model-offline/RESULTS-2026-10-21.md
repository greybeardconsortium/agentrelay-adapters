## Reading it
- **Works:** endpoint, auth and schema fine first try; p50 0.12 s per call with 5 questions; the whole replay cost under 1 cent.
- **ack (Jev 93% vs rules 88%):** Jev is better at long "Acknowledged. I'll keep..." messages (no keyword fixed list). It's stricter than our labels on first votes ("AGREE: Boinglet > ...", counted as a vote in our labels), as is the regex. Missed "Received. Thanks for the snapshot..." (rule missed it too).
- **Stalls:** Jev flagged the 9-min stall at T+9.6 and T+11.8 min and picked `nudge: all` at all 3 stall checkpoints. The 5-min rule only saw state=stalled at the last one.
- **Over-eager "looping":** Jev calls two or three back-to-back agreements a loop (run 2 idx 9–12, 18–19, run 1 idx 13–16), so `stop` fires too early. That makes it unsafe as the only gate on wakes. Use it only as one signal, with a repeat count.
- **Missed post-consensus drift:** run 2 idx 28–29 (claw2/hermes2 agreeing on a new ranking after FINAL CONSENSUS): Jev said progressing / don't stop. The rule (FINAL seen ⇒ done) caught it.
- **nudge (65%):** most misses are `codex2` during run 1 while codex2 refused or held out. Arguably right: the real fix in run 1 was an owner intervention on codex2. Our label "none" is debatable. Jev also wants to nudge right after the brief is posted (no time passed yet).
- **goal_met:** 100% for both, but that's easy here (only run 2 has a FINAL).

## Takeaway
Jev is cheap and fast enough to run on every inbound message. Best use: as the "is this just an ack" and "has this gone quiet / who's stuck" signal, combined with hard rules (repeat count, FINAL/DONE marker, silence timer). Not as the sole loop breaker.
